Every state of the overlay

All 34 states the Walkie Talkie chip and panel can be in — what each one is, and the moment it is on screen. The pictures are the real views drawing themselves, not mockups.

No screen capture can contain this window (sharingType, and RELAY_CAPTURABLE=1 stopped buying it back on macOS 15), so every shot here is RelayWindow.snapshot — the view rendering into a bitmap. Two things that costs: a panel's blur is missing, because the window server draws it and not the view, and the window's alpha is applied here in CSS rather than being in the pixels. The chip has no blur at all, so it comes out exactly as Victor sees it, over whatever is underneath — hence the backdrop switch at the bottom right.

The rule. Any change to the overlay — a new row, a reworded string, a state that appears or disappears — is not finished until this page is rebuilt: ./docs/shoot-overlay-states.sh. A new state means a new entry in Sources/WalkieTalkie/OverlayStates.swift, which is where every word on this page comes from; the page itself is generated and must never be edited by hand.

spawn

Replace Wispr — this one goes to the caret

chipalpha 0.80168×69 pt

The forward side button, while the mode is ticked in the menu: a dictation that is typed where the caret is instead of at an agent.

The caret is a destination like any other, and it takes the line a spawn takes — the bound terminal is still there, and for the length of this sentence the words are not going to it. Nothing else on the chip changes: no shots row, because this mode takes no pictures, and no ⌘⇧ hint, because it borrows nothing from Chrome.

He started typing

no window
no window on screen

Any keystroke, while the chip is on screen and not listening.

Faded to zero — the window is still there, unlike the unbound case, because this lasts as long as a keystroke and a panel ordered out and back would flicker. macOS hides the pointer while typing, and the chip belongs to the pointer. Except while dictating, which is the one state where the chip is the only evidence the microphone is open — and except at a bind, which is a keystroke whose whole answer is drawn beside the pointer, so ⌘⌃B wakes both the chip and the hidden pointer back up before the flight arrives.

typing

The model is coming up

chipalpha 0.80140×46 pt

The ~10 s after a bind, or after the wheel if the weights are not resident.

One row, and the 🎙️ is not on it. The microphone is the readiness, so showing it above a line that says preparing was the chip contradicting itself — and a wheel pressed on the strength of the top line loses the first seconds of the sentence to an engine that is not up. The hourglass is the whole chip until it is; then the microphone appears, and means it. The hourglass is drawn big — 30pt where every other row's glyph is 16: this and transcribing… are the only two states in which Victor is waiting on the app, and the answer to is it still doing something? has to be readable from wherever he looked away to. It was icon-sized and grey, and unreadable on the dark terminals the chip lives over. The words stay at the chip's one size and weight, like every other row — emphasis here is the glyph column's job.

Dictating

chipalpha 0.80169×69 pt

From the wheel click until he clicks it again — the bulk of every dictation.

The destination is named here, and only here — the chip has been a bare 🎙️ all day, and now it wears the destination's own icon too; and the moment the microphone opens it spells out the terminal the words are going to, in time for him to ⌘⌃B somewhere else mid-sentence if it is the wrong one. The pulsing 🔴 says now, Listening… says what. The model id used to follow it and now lives only in the menu: it is a setting, and a setting restated beside the cursor all day pays rent to be read twice a month.

Dictating, at a page

chipalpha 0.80169×93 pt

Dictating and Chrome is the frontmost app.

The one gesture hint the chip still shows. It is worth its pixels because it is only on screen when it is actionable — and because the relay takes ⌘⇧-click away from Chrome while it is up, so a browser that silently stopped opening links would read as broken. The keys and nothing else, since 2026-08-31: the drawn left button that used to follow them was the one glyph on the row he could not act on.

listening-selection-multi

The shutter caught a highlight

chipalpha 0.80353×99 pt

For a beat after every F3 or side-button press that reads a new selection — including one in a Chrome page, which is where it used to read nothing at all.

The verb, then his own words back, on the row that was going to carry them anyway — deliberately not a flash, since a flash is a panel and a shutter press must not throw one over the work he is photographing. It settles to ↪ ×N after 2.5s, and it truncates to whatever width the rows above already gave the chip.

Rare, and the widest the chip ever gets: browser in front, picks made, a highlight riding along.

Four rows beside the cursor. This is the state to look at when a row is added — it is the one that says how much of his screen the chip can cover.

listening-everything

Something went wrong mid-sentence

flashalpha 0.80182×93 pt

A shot that failed, or any flash raised while he is still talking.

The flash takes the last row and the dictation rows stay above it — the chip grows the blur without losing what it was saying. The F3 receipt is deliberately not one of these: taking a picture mid-dictation must not throw a panel across the screen, which is why the highlight it catches is announced on the chip's own row instead.

flash-refused

Dictation cancelled

chipalpha 0.80168×46 pt

The instant the wheel has been held 2 s mid-sentence — 1.5 s, then half a second of dissolve.

Words and nothing else, and bare: no glyph of its own, no lone 🎙️ above it, no blur, no rounded rect, no shadow, no ✕. It is a word replacing a word — it lands in the row Listening… was just occupying, beside the pointer — and a window opening and closing round it for a second and a half read as an event rather than as the state changing back to nothing. The audio is gone; there is nothing to offer him and nothing to undo.

The prompt, under Autosend

panelalpha 1.00369×68 pt

The same moment, with Autosend ticked in the menu — one second, then it is gone.

No Send and no Cancel, and the row they sat on goes with them: two buttons up for one second are two buttons nobody can reach, an invitation to press something that will not be there when the hand arrives.
